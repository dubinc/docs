Dub's Risk Monitoring feature helps you catch and take action on any potentially fraudulent or compliance activity in your program. We give you the details for each event so you can make the best decision for your program and customers.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/dub-partners/risk-table.png" alt="Risk table view" />
</Frame>

The table view contains the following columns:

- **Event:** The specific unique risk event associated with a partner.
- **Partner:** The partner associated with the risk event.
- **Last Detected:** The date and time of the most recent occurrence of this risk event.

Each row is a single event type for a given partner and can contain multiple event occurrences within it. There may be times when you see multiple rows for the same partner, and in this scenario, the partner has been flagged for multiple events that require your review.

## Risk event types

Here are the event types you might see flagged and detected in your program.

### Network-level ban

This partner was banned from one-or-more programs on Dub. Our risk team has reviewed this decision and confirmed the fraudulent behavior or terms of service violation.

This is the strongest indicator that a partner is fraudulent. Network-level bans are manually verified by the Dub team, not flagged automatically by AI, so each one reflects a confirmed case of fraud.

**Recommended action:** Ban these partners from your program.

### Paid traffic

This partner has traffic coming from paid sources like Google Ads, Facebook Ads, etc. and can be a violation of your program's terms. It can also be a sign of fraudulent activity if the partner is running unauthorized ads to generate commissions.

The most common concern is partners bidding on your branded keywords, which competes with your own ads and claims commissions on customers who were already searching for you.

**Recommended action:** If your program doesn't allow paid ads, you can ban the partner outright. Otherwise, ask the partner for details showing that they're not running paid ads on your branded keywords before deciding whether to ban them or resolve the event. To make this easier, you can click the **Request details** button to auto-generate a message to the partner.

### Duplicate account detected

This partner was flagged by our system for having 2 or more Dub accounts. This can be flagged for partners connecting the same payout method, identity, or other personal information provided. Please review to prevent abuse of program restrictions, caps, or bonuses.

A common pattern is a partner who was previously rejected or banned from your program creating a new account to rejoin it.

That said, partners can also create duplicate accounts by mistake. When this happens, we send them a warning email asking them to [merge their accounts](/help/article/merging-partner-accounts#merging-your-partner-accounts) to prevent any future issues. If the accounts are not merged, this can be a sign of self-referral, which is when a partner refers themselves to earn commissions or rewards.

**Recommended action:** Review these events case by case. Check whether any of the associated accounts were previously rejected or banned from your program. If so, the partner is likely trying to get around that decision, and you should ban them.

### Matching customer email

This partner's email address matches with the email address of one or more customers they referred. This can happen in a few ways, and each one should be handled differently:

#### Email address tricks

The partner is referring fake customers using variations of a single email address, such as `dot.em.a.i.l@domain.com`. Many email providers (e.g. Gmail) ignore periods in the address, so `j.o.h.n@gmail.com` and `john@gmail.com` deliver to the same inbox, letting one person sign up as many different "customers".

**Recommended action:** Ban these partners right away.

#### Multiple customers with the same email domain

The partner referred multiple customers who share the same email domain. These can be genuine referrals, for example several people from the same company signing up through the partner's link.

The main red flag is if the email domain doesn't resolve to a real website. This can indicate a "fake company" that the partner is using to generate fraudulent referrals.

**Recommended action:** Review these case by case. Visit the email domain to confirm it belongs to a real company before resolving the event.

#### Self-referral

The partner referred themselves, using their own email address as a customer. This usually happens by accident, such as when a partner tests their own link or signs up for your product through it.

**Recommended action:** There's no need to ban the partner. Instead, cancel the related commissions directly in the risk event review panel by marking them as fraud (see [associated commissions](#associated-commissions)), then resolve the event.

### Suspicious customer email domain

This partner has referred a customer with an email address that uses a suspicious domain, such as a disposable or temporary email provider. This can be a sign of fraudulent activity, as these types of email addresses are often used to create fake accounts or to hide the identity of the referrer.

**Recommended action:** Review the referred customers before deciding. A single customer using a disposable email could be a genuine, privacy-conscious user, so check whether they've made real purchases (e.g. via [Stripe Radar](https://stripe.com/radar)). If the partner has referred several customers with disposable or temporary emails, they are likely fake accounts and you should ban the partner.

### Banned referral source

This partner has traffic coming from a referral source that you've banned in your [risk rules](#risk-rules). This can be a violation of your program's terms if you don't allow traffic from certain sources, and can also be a sign of fraudulent activity if the partner is running unauthorized ads to generate commissions.

**Recommended action:** Confirm the traffic source with the partner via the [messaging center](/help/article/messaging-partners). If the partner wasn't aware of the restriction, ask them to remove their links from that source and resolve the event. If they're knowingly promoting through a source your program doesn't allow, ban them.

## Resolving risk events

When reviewing events, we recommend taking your time and ensuring a proper investigation is done. This could involve:

1. Viewing multiple partner/customer profiles to verify if they are duplicates/self-referrals.
2. Reviewing your internal sales information for any fraud signals (e.g. via [Stripe Radar](https://stripe.com/radar)).
3. Reaching out to the partner in question via our [built-in messaging center](/help/article/messaging-partners) to get clarification on their referral activity.

<Frame>
  <img
    src="https://assets.dub.co/cms/fraud-resolving-event.png"
    alt="Resolving events"
  />
</Frame>

Then, once you've confirmed the veracity of the reports, you can move on to the
next step.

### Associated commissions

For commissions that are on hold for this partner, you can perform the following:

- Click **Mark all as fraud** to mark all commissions on hold as fraud and cancel them.
- You can individually change the commission status to `Fraud` by clicking the `⋯` button and selecting **Edit commission**.
- When [resolving](#resolve-risk-event) the event, any commissions not marked as `Fraud` will move to `Pending`
- When [banning](#ban-partner) the partner, this will change any `On hold` commissions to `Canceled`.

### Resolve risk event

If a risk event is deemed to be benign, you can select the **Resolve event** option to mark the event as safe and keep the partner in your program.

When confirming the decision, you're able to provide optional additional notes about the steps taken to confirm the details, or any other information.

If you trust this partner completely, you're also able to **Exclude from risk monitoring**. When enabled, any future risk events won't be flagged for this partner. This can be changed anytime in the partner's [advanced settings](/help/article/managing-program-partners#advanced-settings).

<Frame>
  <img
    src="/images/dub-partners/risk-resolve-event-modal.png"
    alt="Resolving"
  />
</Frame>

### Ban partner

If the risk event turns out to be malicious, you can select the **Ban partner** option to mark the event as fraudulent and ban the partner from your program.

If the partner has other risk events for review, those events will automatically be resolved as well when the partner is banned.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/dub-partners/fraud-review-ban.png" alt="Ban partner" />
</Frame>
A reason must also be selected from the following options:

| Reason                              | Definition                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Terms of Service Violation          | Partner violated your program rules or Dub’s terms of service.                                  |
| Inappropriate or Offensive Content  | Partner associated your brand or program link with harmful, explicit, or inappropriate content. |
| Artificial Traffic Generation       | Partner used bots, fake clicks, or other methods to inflate traffic.                            |
| Fraudulent Activity                 | Partner generated fake clicks, leads, or other tracked actions to earn rewards.                 |
| Spam or Misleading Content          | Partner used deceptive messaging, false claims, impersonation, or unsolicited promotion.        |
| Brand Abuse or Trademark Violations | Partner misused your brand name, trademarks, or created confusion.                              |

### Reporting fraud events

When banning a partner, you may want to report them to Dub for further investigation or a network ban. To do this, click the toggle **Flag partner for potential fraud**, and provide as much detail as you can about the event.

Our fraud team will review your submission, and if deemed fraudulent, will ban the partner across the entire network and take necessary action to prevent further abuse.

## Risk rules

We give you the option to customize risk signal detection settings on a program-level, as well as choose which [risk event types](#risk-event-types) you want to receive alerts for.

To edit these, click the **Risk rules** button in the top right.

<Frame>
  <img
    src="/images/dub-partners/risk-rules-button.png"
    alt="Risk rules button"
  />
</Frame>

### Paid traffic

If your program doesn't allow traffic from advertisers, ensure this setting is enabled.

<Frame>
  <img
    src="/images/dub-partners/risk-rules-paid-traffic.png"
    alt="Risk rules paid traffic"
  />
</Frame>

When enabled, it will flag all partner traffic that has been generated from several sources, including:

- Google
- Facebook
- X
- Bing
- LinkedIn
- Reddit
- TikTok

#### Google campaign IDs whitelist

Add your internal Google Campaign IDs here to prevent your own Google Ads traffic from being flagged as fraudulent and associated with a partner.

<Frame>
  <img
    src="/images/dub-partners/fraud-settings-google-whitelist.png"
    alt="Google campaign whitelist"
  />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  **Why this matters for Dynamic Search Ads (DSA):** If you run DSA campaigns, Google crawls your site and automatically selects landing pages to serve as ads. Sometimes Google indexes pages that already contain affiliate tracking parameters (e.g., `?via=affiliate`) from affiliate links being shared around the web.

When these pages are served as ads, the affiliate parameter carries through – making it appear that an affiliate is running unauthorized paid ads when it's actually your own campaign. Whitelisting your Google Campaign IDs ensures this internal traffic is correctly attributed to you and not flagged as partner fraud.

</Tip>

### Referral source

If you want to flag traffic from a specific URL, enable this setting and then add the URLs you wish to monitor.

<Frame>
  <img
    src="https://assets.dub.co/cms/fraud-settings-referral.png"
    alt="Referral settings"
  />
</Frame>

When adding URLs, you can also use `*` wildcards to match any part of a domain. These a great when monitoring for a specific page and not all traffic from the specific URL.

### Disabling risk settings

If you don't want to receive alerts for a specific risk event type, you can disable it by toggling the switch to the right of the event type.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/dub-partners/risk-rules.png" alt="Risk rules" />
</Frame>

When you disable any of the current active settings, you're also given the option to mark and pending events that match that criteria as "Resolved".

To do this, check the box labelled "Mark all pending events for this rule as `Resolved`" in the confirmation modal.
